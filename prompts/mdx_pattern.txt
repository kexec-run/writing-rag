Use a document-centered explanatory narrative rather than an investigative diary.

Start with the governing question, tension, or definition that the content evidence supports.
Use observation for that opening question or tension, reframe for a definition or distinction,
finding for a layered textual or technical claim, mechanism for how the distinction produces an
implication, decision for a practical consequence or tradeoff, and conclusion to return to the
governing question.

Layer facts in the order needed for the reader to understand the argument. Do not force personal
scenes, motives, experiments, discarded approaches, or causal connectors when the evidence does
not contain them. The composition pattern controls order; content evidence controls claims; style
evidence controls voice.
